    How to get the files

    Install Overture's command-line tool with pip install overturemaps, then download each theme for a box given as west,south,east,north. For the Santa Barbara front country:

    overturemaps download --bbox=-119.78,34.39,-119.56,34.51 \
  -f geojson --type=segment -o segments.geojson
overturemaps download --bbox=-119.78,34.39,-119.56,34.51 \
  -f geojson --type=building -o buildings.geojson
overturemaps download --bbox=-119.78,34.39,-119.56,34.51 \
  -f geojson --type=land_cover -o landcover.geojson
overturemaps download --bbox=-119.78,34.39,-119.56,34.51 \
  -f geojson --type=land_use -o landuse.geojson

    Make the box about 1–2 km larger than the area you care about; the model trims the edges and uses roads that leave the box as evacuation exits. Keep it under about 25 km across. GeoJSON and GeoJSONSeq both work; for GeoParquet, re-export with -f geojson.

    Without building footprints, households are placed along residential streets. Without land cover, fuels are inferred from terrain and building density. Land use adds irrigated parks, golf courses, orchards and vineyards.

      What the scenarios show

      Runs the same set of random fires through 16 policy scenarios, so the differences come from the policy, not the fire. On a real road network expect roughly 3–10 minutes for 6 fires, depending on the area and your computer; the map pauses while it runs.

      Real inputs: road segments, road classes, names, one-way rules and speed limits, plus building footprints, land cover and land use, come from the Overture Maps Foundation files you upload. Follow Overture's attribution and licensing terms for each theme when you share results. Elevation comes from open Terrain Tiles (AWS Open Data, derived from USGS 3DEP and SRTM). Basemaps are from CARTO, OpenTopoMap, Esri and OpenStreetMap.

      Modeled inputs: residential buildings become households (apartments count as several), sampled so each simulated household can stand for a few homes when the area is large. Evacuation zones are a 2 km grid, not official zones. Fuels come from Overture land cover and land use when you upload them, and are inferred from terrain and building density otherwise; either way they are a coarse stand-in for LANDFIRE fuel models. Exits are where highways and arterials leave the uploaded area. Fire behavior, alert reach, departure delays and road capacities are illustrative and uncalibrated. None of this represents a real event or an official plan.
